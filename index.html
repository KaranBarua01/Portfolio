Thinking & Problem Solving

How I approach difficult problems

Systems thinking

I look beyond the immediate task and map the full system: inputs, handoffs, dependencies, state changes, bottlenecks, failure modes, and downstream effects.

Decomposition

I break large, unclear problems into smaller layers and checkpoints so each part can be understood, tested, and improved without losing the larger objective.

Constraint-first reasoning

I design around real limits such as platform restrictions, budgets, data gaps, access controls, processing limits, deadlines, and operational risk instead of assuming ideal conditions.

Pattern recognition

I look for repeated exceptions, duplicate work, recurring data issues, and hidden rules. Once the pattern is clear, I try to convert it into a reusable system rather than another manual fix.

Fast iteration with safeguards

I prefer short build-test-adjust loops, but with clear gates, backups, validation, and rollback thinking so speed does not come at the cost of reliability.

Business-to-technical translation

I can move from an operational question to process logic, schemas, SQL rules, APIs, automations, QA checks, and user-facing workflows without losing the business context.

Working style

How I build

Start from reality

I begin with how the work actually happens: who does what, where information breaks, what people repeat manually, and what conditions decide the next action.

Make logic explicit

I prefer clear IDs, status models, mappings, rules, data contracts, and health checks over undocumented assumptions or fragile manual knowledge.

Preserve what already works

When improving an existing system, I try to isolate the change, protect stable behavior, and fix the smallest responsible layer instead of rewriting everything unnecessarily.

Keep pushing through ambiguity

I am comfortable working on problems where the final design is not obvious at the start. I refine the model as new constraints and evidence appear.
